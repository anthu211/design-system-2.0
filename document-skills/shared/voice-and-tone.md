# Prevalent AI — Voice and Tone Guidelines

## Brand character

Prevalent AI is an enterprise security platform. All written communication should reflect:

- **Expert** — We speak as security practitioners, not marketers. Use precise terminology.
- **Direct** — Lead with the finding or conclusion, then support it. No burying the lede.
- **Confident, not arrogant** — State positions clearly. Avoid hedging language ("might", "could potentially", "it seems").
- **Concise** — Enterprise readers skim. Every sentence earns its place.

---

## Writing principles

### 1. Lead with the insight
Put the most important information first — in headings, in paragraphs, in bullets. Reports and decks are scanned before they are read.

**Do:** "Third-party vendor access accounts for 43% of critical exposures in Q3."
**Don't:** "After examining the data gathered over the course of Q3, it was found that..."

### 2. Use active voice
**Do:** "The security team remediated 87 vulnerabilities."
**Don't:** "87 vulnerabilities were remediated by the security team."

### 3. Quantify everything you can
Specific numbers build credibility. Vague claims undermine it.

**Do:** "12 vendors exceeded their access window by more than 30 days."
**Don't:** "Several vendors had prolonged access."

### 4. Avoid filler phrases

Omit these entirely:
- "It is important to note that..."
- "Please be advised..."
- "As mentioned above..."
- "In conclusion, we can see that..."
- "Moving forward..."

### 5. Consistency in terminology

Always use:
- **Vendor** (not supplier, partner, or third party when referring to vendors)
- **Risk** (not threat, danger, or hazard unless technically precise)
- **Remediation** (not fixing, addressing, or handling)
- **Exposure** (not vulnerability, weakness, or gap — unless technically specific)
- **Assessment** (not audit, check, or review in formal documents)

## Tone by document type

### Reports (risk, assessment, status)
- Objective, evidence-based, clinical.
- No promotional language.
- Use past tense for findings, present tense for recommendations.
- Recommendation framing: "Prevalent AI recommends..." or "We recommend..."

### Pitch decks / executive presentations
- Confident and strategic — not technical.
- Frame around business outcomes and risk reduction, not features.
- Keep slides punchy: one idea per slide, one sentence per bullet.
- Use active imperatives for recommendations: "Automate vendor attestations" not "Vendor attestations should be automated."

### Memos
- Conversational but professional.
- State purpose in the first sentence.
- Use numbered lists for action items; bullet lists for context.
- Close with a clear next step or decision needed.

## Formatting conventions

- **Headings:** Title case for document titles and section headings. Sentence case for sub-headings and callout labels.
- **Numbers:** Spell out one through nine; use numerals for 10 and above. Always use numerals for percentages (7%, not seven percent) and for data points.
- **Dates:** ISO-ish style — "15 May 2025" or "May 15, 2025" — be consistent within a document. Never "5/15/25".
- **Percentages:** Always include the % symbol. Round to one decimal unless more precision is meaningful.
- **Bullet lists:** Parallel construction. All bullets in a list should be the same grammatical form.
- **Tables:** Every table must have a caption above it. Column headers are uppercase.

---

## What to avoid

- Jargon that isn't standard InfoSec terminology.
- Acronyms without definition on first use (exception: CTA, UI, API in internal docs).
- Exclamation points in formal documents.
- Passive voice in recommendations.
- Sentences longer than 25 words.
- Paragraphs longer than 4 sentences in body copy.
